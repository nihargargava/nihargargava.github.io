Notes from Kristian Holm’s minicourse at RGNT in Lausanne, June 22, 2026. See the programme and lectures 3 and 4. Throughout, \(n \geq 2\).

Lecture 1

Definition. A full-rank lattice in \(\mathbb{R}^{n}\) is a discrete subgroup of the form \(\Lambda = g\mathbb{Z}^{n}\) for some \(g \in \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\). Its covolume is \[ d(\Lambda) = \operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Lambda) = |\det g|. \]

We say that \(\Lambda\) is unimodular if \(d(\Lambda)=1\). Let \[ X_n = \{\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n} : \Lambda \text{ is a lattice and } d(\Lambda)=1\}. \] We identify this space with the homogeneous space \[ X_n \simeq \mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}). \]

There are many interesting questions:

  1. How many sublattices \(\Pi \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^{n}\) have index \([\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\Pi]=N\)?
  2. How short can non-zero lattice vectors be? This is related to short closed geodesics on \(\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Lambda\) and to lattice-based cryptography.
  3. Given a set \(S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}\), estimate \(\#(\Lambda \cap S)\).
Lattice or integral representation Region Related problem
\(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\) \(B_0(R)\) Gauss circle problem
\(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\) \(\{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2}:xy\leq T\}\) Dirichlet divisor problem
Integral binary cubic forms Regions of bounded absolute discriminant Davenport–Heilbronn theorem, after taking orbits and imposing the appropriate arithmetic conditions

The Davenport–Heilbronn theorem counts cubic fields up to isomorphism. With both discriminant signs included, the normalization is \[ \#\{K/\mathbb{Q} : [K:\mathbb{Q}]=3,\ |\operatorname{disc}(K)|\leq T\} \sim \frac{T}{3\zeta(3)}. \] See Bhargava–Shankar–Tsimerman, Theorem 1 for the separate constants for positive and negative discriminants.

Sphere packings

If \(\Lambda \cap B_0(1)=\{0\}\), with \(B_0(1)\) the open unit ball, then the unit balls centred at \(2\Lambda\) form a lattice packing. Its density is \[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_0(1))}{2^{n}d(\Lambda)}. \]

Minkowski–Hlawka theorem (1943). Let \(S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}\) be a measurable, centrally symmetric set that is star-shaped about \(0\). If \[ \operatorname{vol}(S)<2\zeta(n), \] then there exists \(\Lambda \in X_n\) such that \(\Lambda \cap S \subseteq \{0\}\).

For \(f \in C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\), its Siegel transform is the function \(\widehat{f}:X_n\to\mathbb{C}\) defined by \[ \widehat{f}(\Lambda)=\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v). \]

Siegel’s mean value theorem (1945). Let \(\mu\) be the invariant probability measure on \(X_n\). Then \(\widehat{f}\in L^1(X_n,\mu)\) and \[ \int_{X_n}\widehat{f}(\Lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\Lambda) =\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. \]

The formula also applies to \(f=\mathbf{1}_S\) for a measurable set \(S\) of finite volume, giving \[ \int_{X_n}\#\bigl((\Lambda\setminus\{0\})\cap S\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\Lambda) =\operatorname{vol}(S). \] To obtain the extra factor of \(\zeta(n)\), one sieves for primitive points.

Definition. A non-zero vector \(v\in\Lambda\) is primitive if \[ \Lambda\cap\{tv:0\leq t\leq1\}=\{0,v\}. \]

Primitive lattice points have asymptotic density \(\zeta(n)^{-1}\) among all lattice points.

Lecture 2

There is a natural measure on \(X_n\) with total mass \[ \widetilde{\mu}(X_n)=\frac{1}{n}\zeta(2)\zeta(3)\cdots\zeta(n). \] Thus \(\mu=\widetilde{\mu}(X_n)^{-1}\widetilde{\mu}\) is a probability measure.

Write \(G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n^2}\). For a measurable set \(A\subseteq G\), define \[ C_A(R)=\{tg:g\in A,\ 0<t\leq R\}. \] The cone normalization of Haar measure is \[ \widetilde{\mu}(A)=\operatorname{Leb}_{n^2}(C_A(1)). \] Let \(\mathcal{F}\subseteq G\) be a fundamental domain for the right action of \(\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\). Scaling the cone gives \[ \widetilde{\mu}(X_n) =R^{-n^2}\operatorname{Leb}_{n^2}(C_{\mathcal{F}}(R)). \]

Define \(L_n(m)\) to be the number of sublattices \(\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\) with \([\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\Pi]=m\). Every full-rank integer matrix with positive determinant spans a sublattice, and choosing its normalized representative in \(\mathcal{F}\) selects one positively oriented basis for that sublattice. Consequently, \[ \#\bigl(\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cap C_{\mathcal{F}}(R)\bigr) =\sum_{m\leq R^n}L_n(m). \] This suggests computing the cone volume through \[ R^{-n^2}\sum_{m\leq R^n}L_n(m). \] The passage from lattice-point counts to volume requires justification because the fundamental domain is unbounded; that step is not recorded in these notes.

Counting sublattices

Every sublattice \(\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\) has a basis in Hermite normal form: \[ \Pi= \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0\\ * & d_2 & \cdots & 0\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ * & * & \cdots & d_n \end{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{n}. \] Here \(d_i>0\), the entries below the diagonal in row \(i\) lie in \(\{0,\ldots,d_i-1\}\), and \(d_1\cdots d_n=m\) for an index-\(m\) sublattice. The number of choices for the stars is \[ d_1^0d_2^1d_3^2\cdots d_n^{n-1}. \] Therefore \[ L_n(m)=\sum_{d_1\cdots d_n=m}d_1^0d_2^1\cdots d_n^{n-1}. \]

This is related to Dirichlet convolution: \[ \left(\sum_{r\geq1}\frac{a_r}{r^s}\right) \left(\sum_{t\geq1}\frac{b_t}{t^s}\right) =\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{\sum_{rt=m}a_rb_t}{m^s}, \] in a region of absolute convergence. In particular, \[ \sum_{m\geq1}\frac{L_n(m)}{m^s} =\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)\cdots\zeta(s-n+1),\qquad \operatorname{Re}(s)>n. \] The rightmost pole is at \(s=n\). The Wiener–Ikehara Tauberian theorem gives \[ \sum_{m\leq X}L_n(m) \sim\frac{X^n}{n}\operatorname*{Res}_{s=n} \bigl(\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)\cdots\zeta(s-n+1)\bigr) =\frac{X^n}{n}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\zeta(j). \]

Hecke operators

Definition. For \(F\in L^2(X_n)\) and a prime \(p\), the Hecke operator \(T_p\) is \[ (T_pF)(g\mathbb{Z}^{n}) =\frac{1}{L_n(p)}\sum_{c=1}^{n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_p^{c-1}} F\bigl(p^{-1/n}gM_c(x)\mathbb{Z}^{n}\bigr), \] where, using integer representatives of the row vector \(x\), \[ M_c(x)= \begin{pmatrix} I_{c-1} & 0 & 0\\ x & p & 0\\ 0 & 0 & I_{n-c} \end{pmatrix}. \] Empty blocks are omitted when \(c=1\) or \(c=n\).

The matrices \(M_c(x)\) are exactly the Hermite normal forms of determinant \(p\). In particular, \[ L_n(p)=1+p+\cdots+p^{n-1}. \]

Two useful facts are:

  1. The probability measure \(\mu\) is invariant under \(T_p\): \[ \int_{X_n}T_pF\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{X_n}F\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \]
  2. If \(F\in L^2(X_n)\) and \(T_pF\) converges almost everywhere to a constant as \(p\to\infty\) through the primes, then that constant is \(\int_{X_n}F\,\mathrm{d}\mu\). The \(L^2\) contraction property gives the uniform integrability needed to pass to the limit.

Conceptually, \(T_p\) averages over the unimodular rescalings of the index-\(p\) sublattices of a fixed lattice \(\Lambda\): \[ (T_pF)(\Lambda) =\frac{1}{L_n(p)} \sum_{\substack{\Pi\subseteq\Lambda\\{}[\Lambda:\Pi]=p}}F(p^{-1/n}\Pi). \]
